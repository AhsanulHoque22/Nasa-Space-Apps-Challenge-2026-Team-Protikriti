## What the AI did
- Wrote first drafts of most TypeScript and Python code, and its unit tests, under the standards in `CLAUDE.md` (tests first, strict type checks).
- Wrote the OpenCV panorama step (`pipeline/src/marsmap/cahvore.py`, `navcam.py`, `pano.py`, `streetview.py`) and its tests, after watching a public OpenCV image-stitching tutorial (Nicolai Nielsen, YouTube) the team pointed it to.
- Drafted documentation: `README.md`, `docs/data-sources.md`, the demo script and the pitch script.
- One language model runs inside the app, and only to explain: **Ask ATLAS** (since 2026-10-31), a side panel on the map and in Story mode. It calls Claude Haiku 5.5 (Anthropic) through our server function (`api/explain.ts`, logic in `web/src/server/explain.ts`); the API key lives in the hosting settings, never in the repo or the browser. The app builds the facts it may use from its own tested code (`web/src/core/explain-context.ts`: the route, the view centre, the saved Story run, the stores ledger and the numbers on the open step), and its instructions forbid computing or inventing numbers and require a citation [S#] for each. Every answer is then checked by ordinary code (`web/src/core/explain-check.ts`): each number in it must be one of the app's numbers (or the player's own), and any that is not is shown as "not from the app's data". Routing, slope, Mars time, verdicts and panorama stitching remain ordinary tested code (`web/src/core/`, `pipeline/`); no figure in the app comes from the model. Without a network or a key the panel says it is offline and the app works as before. Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-ask-atlas-explainer-design.md`.
- One trained model exists, offline: a scikit-learn Random Forest in `pipeline/src/marsmap/classify.py` that we train on NASA's AI4Mars human labels to complete the terrain labels on whole panoramas (method and limits in `docs/data-sources.md`). It is our own small model, not a language model and not a third-party one. The app only shows its precomputed output, and no route, slope or other figure depends on it.
- The 25 cm HiRISE walk-patch tiles are sharpened for display with classical OpenCV filters only (`pipeline/src/marsmap/enhance.py`). No super-resolution or generative model is used, so no terrain is invented.
- The tidied HiRISE strips on the globe use classical OpenCV and SciPy only (Hough-free oriented seam detection, Poisson re-integration, phase correlation to CTX): no learned or generative model, so no terrain is invented (`pipeline/src/marsmap/strips.py`).
- Claude wrote the code for that model, the sky and rover masks, and the rock detector; the team chose to use AI4Mars labels and reviewed the output over the panoramas.
- The Colony Intelligence Layer (6-channel suitability grid, resource budget calculator, 3D module placement): Claude wrote the pipeline (`wind_stats.py`, `colony_score.py`), the TypeScript core (`colony-score.ts`, `colony-budget.ts`, `colony-modules.ts`) and the UI panels (`colony-panel.ts`, `colony-summary-panel.ts`). Every number traces to a NASA dataset or published constant (Appelbaum & Flood 1990, Hecht et al. 2021, Morgan et al. 2021, NASA HRP baseline). No AI model computes a colony number at runtime; all scoring runs in the Python pipeline, producing `colony_score.bin`. The proxy GLB models (`make_proxy_models.py`) are geometric primitives only, generated by trimesh.
- Story mode (start screen, local profile, mission list, mission 1.1 cargo packing): Claude did the research and wrote the design specs (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-25-*`), the TypeScript core (`profile.ts`, `local-store.ts`, `missions.ts`, `mission-progress.ts`, `mission-constants.ts`, `cargo.ts`, `assumptions.ts`) and the screens (`profile-gate.ts`, `story.ts`, `mission-list.ts`, `missions/cargo-screen.ts`). Every mass and rate comes from NASA documents (DRA 5.0, Rucker et al. 2016, BVAD Rev2), cited in `docs/data-sources.md`; one value (oxygen plant mass) is derived from a stated percentage and labelled as derived on screen. The team chose the storyline, the crew of four, the NASA baseline, the 40 t lander and the mission order. No AI model runs in Story mode.
- Mission 1.2 (landing site, cargo-phase verdict): Claude found and extracted the Appelbaum & Flood net flux table, wrote the sunlight model (`surface-solar.ts`, `netflux-table.ts`), the cargo-phase energy balance (`cargo-phase.ts`), zone judging (`landing-sites.ts`) and the screen (`missions/site-screen.ts`). It checked the model against Rucker et al.'s own demonstrator. The verdict is ordinary tested code; no AI computes it.
- Mission 1.1 journey (3D): at the team's request (inspired by an ESA figure), Claude wrote the planet ephemeris (`ephemeris.ts`), the Lambert solver (`lambert.ts`), the transfer search (`journey.ts`, run in `journey.worker.ts`) and the three.js scene (`journey-scene.ts`). Dates, days, Δv, propellant, dose and supplies are computed; nothing is drawn by an AI model.
- Mission 1.1 cargo bay: Claude optimised two CC BY 4.0 Sketchfab models from the team's folder (glTF-Transform) and wrote the three.js bay (`cargo-bay.ts`), the side-by-side layout and the live charts (`charts.ts`, `cargo-views.ts`, `power-profile.ts`). The team chose a habitat plus cargo containers after no lander model was found, then chose NASA's Habitat Demonstration Unit from NASA 3D Resources after rejecting the MDRS habitat and the Mars One base. The team then chose the Jezero terrain model as the ground and the Sketchfab embed of the MDRS HAB for the full-screen habitat view; Claude wrote the terrain placement and `habitat-viewer.ts`.
- Mission 1.2 map visit: at the team's request Claude researched each certified site's points of interest (web searches of NASA, JPL, PDS, Wikipedia and science-news sources, each cited in `site-features.ts`), located every pin from data (NASA rover-stop metadata, the PDS Spirit traverse, the site terrain models) and wrote the pins layer (`feature-pins.ts`) and the guided steps (`site-steps.ts`). Claude computed sols from dates and distances from positions; no position was placed by eye. Claude also found and fixed map tools reacting to terrain clicks during a mission (`isExploring`). At the team's request Claude then found one NASA photo per pin through the NASA Image and Video Library API (rejecting computer-generated and 3-D images), wrote the captions from NASA's own descriptions, and drew the zone chart (`zoneChart` in `charts.ts`).
- Flight Director: at the team's request Claude wrote `core/flight-director.ts` (a search over small cargo changes, each checked with the cargo-phase model) and its help panel. The team set the rules: shown only after a NO-GO and only when asked, options only, never a full plan. No language model is involved; a chatbot explainer is planned for a later phase.
- Mission 1.3 Landing: Claude found the NASA onboard landing video, captions and stage photos through the NASA Image and Video Library API, re-encoded the video with ffmpeg, sourced the facts (NASA PIA24285, NTRS 20100026017, NASA Glenn atmosphere model) and wrote `mars-atmosphere.ts`, `landing-steps.ts`, `landing-stage.ts` and the pressure chart. The team chose the full-length video, the Habitat Lander Pod model and a Starship model as an illustration of the cargo rocket.
- Mission 1.4 Build the base: Claude confirmed the 1 km reactor distance in Rucker et al. 2016's text and wrote the placement rules and terrain-shadow check (`core/base-layout.ts`, tested on synthetic grids), the map placer (`map/base-placer.ts`) and the steps (`base-steps.ts`). The team chose the 15° slope limit as a labelled team assumption and to add the Phobos transit event later.

## Terrain label accuracy (held-out stops)
Measured by training on 4 of every 5 stops and scoring the fifth (`classify-panoramas --evaluate`). Share of each class's true pixels the forest finds, Perseverance: soil 51%, bedrock 50%, sand 48%, big rock 33% (rocks are 0.4% of labelled pixels). That is modest: about half of each class. Curiosity figures are in `data/pano/msl_labels.log` after a run. Rocks shown in the app also come from a contrast rule (`docs/data-sources.md`) that has not been scored against people's labels.

It chose the pressurised rover model from the team's folder and compressed it with glTF-Transform. Every number is sourced or marked as a team assumption in `docs/data-sources.md`. The ground grain is drawn, not measured, and the tablet says so.
